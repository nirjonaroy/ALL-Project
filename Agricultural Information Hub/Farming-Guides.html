Strategic Farming Methodologies

Methodologies dictate the high-level philosophy and operational design of a farm.
The hub categorizes these to help farmers choose systems that match their local climate, soil conditions, and target markets.

Conservation Agriculture
  • Zero-Tillage: Eliminates traditional plowing to preserve soil structure, prevent erosion, and trap carbon in the soil.
  • Crop Residue Retention: Leaves stalks and leaves from previous harvests on the field to act as a natural mulch layer.
  • Strategic Rotation: Alternates deep-rooting crops with shallow-rooting varieties to prevent nutrient depletion in specific soil zones.
Climate-Smart Agriculture
  • Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD): Drops water use in rice fields by up to 30% by letting the soil dry before re-flooding.
  • Agroforestry Integration: Plants high-value trees alongside field crops to create windbreaks, shield crops from heat, and generate extra income.
  • Stress-Resilient Sowing: Uses specific crop varieties bred to survive heavy flash floods, prolonged dry spells, or high soil salinity.
Climate-Smart Agriculture
  • Closed-Loop Nutrient Cycles: Replaces synthetic inputs with farm-made compost, bio-slurry, and green manure crops like dhaincha or alfalfa.
  • Biological Biomass Building: Uses cover crops during fallow periods to fix nitrogen naturally and suppress weed growth without chemicals.
