To build a high-utility database for the Agricultural Information Hub, crops are systematically categorized across four critical layers: soil requirements, crops varieties, cultivation methods, and harvesting techniques.

Soil Texture

  • Sandy Soils: High drainage, low nutrient retention. Ideal for root crops like potatoes and groundnuts.
  • Clay Soils: High nutrient retention, poor drainage, heavy compaction. Ideal for water-saturated crops like wetland rice.
  • Loamy Soils: The ideal agricultural balance of sand, silt, and clay. Offers excellent drainage and nutrient holding capacity for most vegetables and cereals.

Soil Reaction / pH Level

  • Acidic (pH < 6.0): Often found in high-rainfall or tea-growing regions (e.g., Sylhet). Restricts phosphorus and calcium uptake.
  • Neutral (pH 6.0 – 7.5): The optimal zone where primary macronutrients (N, P, K) are highly soluble and bioavailable.
  • Alkaline/Saline (pH > 7.5): Common in coastal zones (e.g., Khulna, Satkhira). High salt concentrations cause osmotic stress, stunting root systems.

Organic Matter Content

  • The Baseline: Healthy agricultural soil requires a minimum of 2.5% to 3.0% organic matter.
  • The Reality: Many intensively farmed subtropical soils have dropped below 1.0% to 1.5%, requiring immediate regenerative interventions like green manuring or biochar integration.

Electrical Conductivity

  • Measures soil salinity, measured in deciSiemens per meter (dS/m).
  • Crucial for coastal delta hubs to flag shifting saltwater intrusion zones and trigger alerts for salt-tolerant crop varieties.

Boro Rice

  • BRRI dhan28 & BRRI dhan29: Historically dominant national staples; high yields but increasingly susceptible to blast disease in older seed lineages.
  • BRRI dhan89 & BRRI dhan92: Modern, high-yielding replacements outperforming older lines with stronger straw structure and better disease resistance.

Aman Rice

  • BRRI dhan87 & BRRI dhan49: Characterized by fine grains, high milling recovery, and superior yield profiles during the monsoon cycle.

Hybrid Maize

  • NK-40 & Pioneer P3355: Exceptional grain yield potential, widely utilized for the expanding domestic poultry and cattle feed industries.
Direct Seeded Rice
  • Eliminates the traditional nursery phase and manual transplanting entirely.
  • Pre-germinated seeds are sown directly into puddle fields using a manual drum seeder or mechanical tractor attachment.
  • Resource Savings: Cuts labor requirements by up to 50% and reduces methane emissions by lowering early-season water needs.
System of Rice Intensification
  • A methodology based on changing the management of plants, soil, water, and nutrients.
  • Single, young seedlings (8–12 days old) are transplanted carefully at wide, precise intervals (25cm x 25cm) rather than dense clumps of older seedlings.
  • Resource Savings: Reduces seed requirements by up to 75% and irrigation water by 30–40% while boosting root development.
Raised Bed Planting System
  • Crops like maize, wheat, and vegetables are planted on elevated trapezoidal beds, while irrigation water flows through the channels or furrows between them.
  • Resource Savings: Prevents root drowning during sudden heavy rain, reduces water consumption by up to 30%, and lowers soil compaction
Cereal Grains

Trigger harvest when 80–85% of the panicles turn a golden yellow color. Grain moisture content should ideally be checked using a handheld meter, targeting 20–24% moisture for mechanical harvesting.

Root Crops

Perform "haulm pulling" (cutting the top green leaves) 7–10 days before digging out the tubers. This hardens the potato skin, protecting it from bruising during transport.

Horticultural Crops

Follow strict color charts depending on the distance to the target market. Use the "Breaker Stage" (first pink blush) for long-distance transport and the "Red Ripe Stage" for immediate local sales.
